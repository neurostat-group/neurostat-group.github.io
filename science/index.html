Science

We start with questions neuroscience has not yet answered.

Our approach

Most of our programmes started from a question nobody had answered well. Why does a memory that is still stored become impossible to reach? Why does thinking fall apart in a predictable order when sleep is cut short? Why do some people adapt to night shifts and others never do? We pick questions where a better answer would change treatment, then test the answer on the people who live with the problem.

That often takes us to demanding places: offshore installations, polar stations, desert sites and military bases, and also hospital wards and sleep laboratories. People there are exposed to sleep loss, isolation and a disrupted body clock in ways that are easy to repeat and to measure. Access and logistics at these sites are handled by our group companies Hiemal Polar Services, Eremos Desert Operations and Aegis Corporation.

Research area

Sleep & Circadian Health

Sleep is governed by two systems. One is a pressure that builds the longer we stay awake. The other is a clock in the suprachiasmatic nucleus that decides when we are ready to sleep. Shift work, travel and long polar rotations pull the two apart.

Vesperil acts on the melatonin receptors MT1 and MT2 and helps with sleep timing. Lunavel blocks the orexin receptors and so lowers the drive to stay awake. At the moment we are working on resetting the clock after sudden shifts (NS-455) and on the grogginess that follows waking from deep sleep (NS-318).

Research area

Memory & Cognition

Forgetting is sometimes a storage problem and sometimes a retrieval problem. After some head injuries and after severe stress, the evidence points to memories that are still stored but can no longer be reached. In animals, such memories have been recovered by reactivating the neurons that hold them, the memory engram.

NS-091 (retrevamide) builds on that idea. It acts on a persistent molecular signature of silent engrams and reactivates only the group of neurons that holds the lost memory, leaving other memories alone. It is in a Phase III study in patients with retrograde amnesia after trauma.

NS-760 (adravacetam) is aimed at a different problem: keeping attention and working memory steady through long isolation and broken sleep. It is being tested in BOREAS-2.

Research area

Neurodiagnostics

Measuring sleep and cognition usually means a trip to a laboratory. Halo and Baseline bring the measurement to the workplace. Halo records EEG with dry electrodes. Baseline compares attention, memory and decision making with the same person's earlier results. Occupational physicians use both, and the data feed back into our research. In our own studies we also use Fathom, a whole-body imaging system in which the person floats in a tank of breathable fluid while sound waves scan the whole body.
