Medicine

Vesperil

Helps shift workers fall asleep at the wrong time of day.

What it is

Active substanceormelteon
ClassMelatonin MT1/MT2 receptor agonist
IndicationInsomnia associated with shift work disorder in adults
First approved2006

How it works

Ormelteon binds to the MT1 and MT2 melatonin receptors in the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the brain's master clock. MT1 is linked to falling asleep, MT2 to shifting the phase of the daily rhythm. It has no measurable effect on GABA receptors, so it is not a sedative-hypnotic in the usual sense.

Important safety information

  • Common side effects: sleepiness, dizziness, fatigue and nausea.
  • Do not combine with fluvoxamine or other strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. They raise ormelteon levels many times over.
  • Not recommended in severe liver impairment, and to be used with caution in moderate impairment.
  • Changes in prolactin and testosterone have been reported. Consider testing if a patient has absent periods, milk production, reduced libido or fertility problems.
  • Patients should not drive or use machinery until they know how Vesperil affects them.

This is a summary. It does not list every risk. Read the package leaflet before use and ask a healthcare professional if you have questions.

Clinical information

Are you a healthcare professional?

The clinical information below is intended for healthcare professionals.

No

  • Approval rested on randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies in adults working rotating or night shifts.
  • The primary endpoint was total sleep time in the daytime sleep period, measured by polysomnography and sleep diary.
  • Compared with placebo, Vesperil increased total sleep time and shortened the time to fall asleep, and the effect held through the six-month extension.
  • Generic ormelteon reached several markets in 2026.

Suspected adverse reactions should be reported to the national authority. You can also contact NeuroStat medical information.
